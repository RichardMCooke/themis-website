Tax planning for UK SMEs: timing, reliefs and the decisions that matter

Almost every planning point here is a timing point. Very few reduce the total tax paid — most change when it is paid, and what it costs in cash along the way.

Briefing

Technical briefing · September 2026

Good tax planning for a UK SME is rarely about clever structuring. In practice it is about getting the timing right on decisions the business would be making anyway: when to invest in equipment, when to invest in research and development, how and when directors take money out of the company, when to declare a dividend, and when to start thinking seriously about succession.

Two Budgets in the last two years, together with reforms already legislated for 2026, have moved most of the dials that owner-managed businesses rely on. Capital allowances, R&D relief, the charge on overdrawn director’s loan accounts, dividend tax rates, employee share schemes, Business Asset Disposal Relief and Business Property Relief have all changed. Several changed twice.

Why the distinction matters

Full expensing is a timing difference rather than a permanent saving — it accelerates relief that would otherwise arrive over several years, and it is clawed back on disposal. The value is in the cash flow and in the rate at which relief is obtained, not in the headline percentage. The same is true of loss relief, instalment payments and most extraction planning.

1 · Timing capital expenditure

01

The three routes to 100% relief

  • AIA — £1m a year. Companies, sole traders and most partnerships; new and second-hand assets; now a permanent feature.
  • Full expensing — uncapped. Companies only, new and unused main-pool plant and machinery. Not assets bought for leasing, not cars, not second-hand.
  • 50% FYA on new special rate expenditure.
  • Sequence the claims. Point AIA at expenditure that would otherwise attract only the special rate, since full expensing cannot reach it at 100%.

Why it matters

Run together properly, a company can get 100% on up to £1m of second-hand or special-rate spend through AIA while using uncapped full expensing for new main-pool assets.

02

Two changes that alter the calculus

  • From 1 January 2026, a new 40% first-year allowance applies to qualifying main-rate plant and machinery not covered by AIA or full expensing.
  • From 1 April 2026, the main pool writing-down allowance falls from 18% to 14%. Expenditure left in the general pool now takes roughly a quarter longer to write down.
  • Straddling periods are calculated on a blended basis, apportioned by days either side of the change.

Why it matters

The slower the pool unwinds, the more the year in which you buy is worth arguing about.

03

The disposal trap

  • Full expensing carries a clawback AIA does not. Disposal proceeds come in as an immediate balancing charge under CAA 2001 s59A, not as a reduction of the pool.
  • Claimed on the whole cost? The charge equals the whole disposal value. On part, a proportion applies. On 50% FYA assets, half the disposal value.
  • For kit you’ll sell on soon, AIA or writing-down allowances may leave the company better off.
  • The fixed asset register becomes compliance, not housekeeping — cost, date, and which allowance was claimed.

Why it matters

Front-loading relief that is then clawed back in full is not a saving. It is a loan.

Buildings

Non-residential construction, renovation and conversion falls outside the plant and machinery regime and attracts the Structures and Buildings Allowance: 3% straight line over 33⅓ years, on contracts from 29 October 2018. No first-year relief, no acceleration — the only timing decision is when the building is brought into use. An allowance statement is a statutory requirement, and a buyer of a used structure must obtain a copy from the previous owner. On any project, have the spend analysed between the structure and the plant embedded in it — heating, lighting, ventilation, sanitary ware. The plant element reaches 100%; the structure crawls at 3%. The split is often worth several times the cost of the exercise.

Vehicles

Cars are excluded from both AIA and full expensing, with one exception: new and unused zero-emission cars qualify for a 100% first-year allowance, as does expenditure on charge points — now running to 31 March 2027 for corporation tax and 5 April 2027 for income tax. Used electric cars go into the main pool at 14%. The benefit-in-kind position is correspondingly favourable: 4% for a fully electric company car in 2026/27, rising to 5%, 7% and 9% by 2029/30, against rates approaching 37% for conventional cars. A 100% deduction plus a single-figure benefit charge remains one of the more attractive reliefs available.

2 · R&D and the Patent Box

Merged scheme or ERIS?

Model it before the year end, not after

  • Merged R&D Expenditure Credit. 20% above the line, worth c.14.7p–16.2p per £1 after tax, any size of company. Loss-makers suffer notional tax at 19%, which is why the net rate lands at 16.2p.
  • ERIS. Loss-making SMEs at 30%+ R&D intensity: 86% additional deduction, loss surrendered for a 14.5% payable credit — up to c.27p per £1. A one-year grace period applies if intensity temporarily drops.
  • ERIS is not automatically better. The 27p assumes losses to surrender. Near break-even there is little to surrender, and the flat merged rate can win.
  • PAYE cap. Cash is capped at £20,000 plus 300% of relevant PAYE and NIC — biting hardest where R&D is delivered by subcontractors or low-salaried directors.
  • Contracted-out R&D can now be included regardless of who the work goes to, subject to conditions, restricted to 65% where the third party is unconnected. Often missed.
  • Overseas costs — subcontractors and externally provided workers outside the UK — have been restricted since April 2024, subject to exemptions.

Compliance timing now matters as much as the claim

Missed dates lose good claims

  • Six-month advance notification. First-time claimants, or anyone who has not claimed in three years, must notify HMRC within six months of the period end. Miss it and the claim is lost outright, regardless of merit.
  • Additional Information Form with or before the CT600, naming a senior officer and setting out projects and costs.
  • Contemporaneous documentation of the technological uncertainty being resolved. HMRC’s compliance campaign means a claim that once passed unexamined no longer does.
  • Patent Box. A 10% effective rate on profits from qualifying patents, against a 25% main rate — complementary to R&D relief, not an alternative. The nexus fraction limits the benefit to R&D you performed yourself or subcontracted to unconnected parties. Election applies from the start of the period in which it is made. For an SME that files patents and does not claim it, this is frequently the largest unclaimed relief on the balance sheet.

3 · Director’s loans — two charges, different triggers

01

Section 455 — 35.75%

Charged on a close company where a loan to a participator is still outstanding nine months and one day after the period end. The rate tracks the higher dividend rate: 33.75% to 5 April 2026, 35.75% after.

Repayable — but slowly. The claim cannot be made until nine months after the end of the period in which the repayment falls, and the money arrives nine months and one day after that period end. Claims must be made within four years. Interest charged on the s455 liability is not recoverable.

It cannot be timed around: a repayment of £5,000 or more followed by a new advance within 30 days is disregarded, and a broader arrangements rule catches repayments always intended to be redrawn. A properly accrued and paid bonus is a payment of earnings and can clear a loan account cleanly.

02

Beneficial loan interest

A separate charge arises where the balance exceeds £10,000 at any point in the tax year. Charge interest below HMRC’s official rate — 3.75% from 6 April 2025, reviewed quarterly — and the shortfall is a taxable benefit: income tax for the director, Class 1A at 15% for the company, reported on P11D.

Charging at or above the official rate removes this exposure entirely, and has no bearing at all on the section 455 position. It is perfectly possible to have both charges on the same loan account.

The most common and most expensive error here is treating the director’s loan account as a year-end problem. By the time the accounts are being prepared, the options have already narrowed.

4 · Profit extraction: dividends, bonuses, pensions

Dividends

From 6 April 2026 the basic rate rose from 8.75% to 10.75% and the higher rate from 33.75% to 35.75%. The additional rate is unchanged at 39.35% and the £500 allowance is unchanged. Personal allowance, higher rate and additional rate thresholds are frozen until April 2031, so fiscal drag keeps pushing dividend income into higher bands without any further rate change.

Why it matters

Because s455 tracks the higher dividend rate, the same measure that made dividends dearer also made leaving a loan account overdrawn dearer.

Bonuses and the nine-month rule

Remuneration accrued at a period end is deductible in that period only if actually paid within nine months (CTA 2009 s1288). Paid later, the deduction moves to the period of payment — and where the computation is prepared before the nine months expire and the amount is still unpaid, it must be added back and the return amended later if it is paid.

This catches holiday pay accruals and unallocated bonus pools, not just director bonuses. PAYE runs on the earliest of several dates, so the PAYE period and the corporation tax deduction period can easily fall in different years.

The discipline

Hold a board meeting before the year end recording the bonus, accrue it, and pay it within nine months.

Pensions

Deductible subject to wholly-and-exclusively, and outside employer NIC entirely — unlike salary or bonus. Annual allowance £60,000 for 2025/26 and 2026/27, tapered by £1 for every £2 of adjusted income above £260,000 to a £10,000 floor, with a £200,000 threshold-income gateway. Three years of unused allowance can be carried forward.

The timing rule is stricter than for most expenses and regularly missed: under FA 2004 s196 relief is available only for contributions actually paid in the period, in cleared funds. The money must physically leave the account before the year end.

Contributions above 210% of the prior period can be spread forward over up to four periods. And from 6 April 2029, NIC relief on salary-sacrificed contributions will be capped at the first £2,000 a year.

Why it matters

On a large catch-up contribution the cash leaves in one year while the relief may not. Model it before committing.

5 · Corporation tax, thresholds and payment timing

The marginal relief band

19% to £50,000, 25% above £250,000, marginal relief in between — an effective 26.5% on each additional pound inside the band, higher than the main rate itself. A company inside the band gets 26.5p in the pound from deductible spend, against 25p above it. Conversely, dropping below £50,000 saves at 19% on everything below that point.

Associated companies

The £50,000 and £250,000 limits, the instalment thresholds and the £5m loss deductions allowance are all divided by associated companies plus one. The test replaced the older 51% group test from 1 April 2023 and is broader — common control, not shareholding percentages. Dormant companies are generally excluded. One forgotten trading company can halve the thresholds for everything else.

Instalments

“Large” above £1.5m of augmented profits, “very large” above £20m — divided by associated companies, so a company in a group of four is caught at £375,000. A year of grace on first breach if profits stay under £10m; none for very large. Large-company instalments fall in months 7, 10, 13 and 16; very large in months 3, 6, 9 and 12 — entirely within the year being taxed. In the transition year the previous lump sum and a new instalment can land weeks apart.

6 · Losses, VAT, employment costs and EMI

Losses are a rate-arbitrage question

Same period, carried back 12 months, or forward indefinitely — and three years for terminal losses. Nothing is automatic: a claim is needed, and carry-back has a two-year deadline. Post-April 2017 losses can go against total profits, subject to a £5m deductions allowance and 50% above it; the return must specify the allowance, or relief is restricted to 50% even where losses are far below the threshold. Pre-2017 losses are same-trade only and used automatically. A loss relieved at 26.5% is worth more than the same loss relieved at 19%.

VAT registration is a rolling test

£90,000 of taxable turnover on a backward-looking rolling 12 months, checked at each month end, plus a forward test for the next 30 days alone. Deregistration at £88,000. The rolling test catches businesses out because it resets monthly, not annually — notify within 30 days of the month end, registration bites from the first day of the second month after. Early voluntary registration can pay where customers are VAT-registered and there is input VAT to recover on planned capital spend.

Employment costs after April 2025

Employer NIC at 15% and a secondary threshold of £5,000 made every employee and every pay rise dearer. Against that, the Employment Allowance doubled to £10,500 and the £100,000 eligibility cap went, so most SMEs can claim it in full — single-director companies still cannot. It is not automatic and must be claimed through payroll each year; worth confirming rather than assuming. At 15%, pension salary sacrifice saves £150 per £1,000 sacrificed in employer NIC alone.

EMI was widened from April 2026

Employee limit up from 250 to 500 FTE; gross assets from £30m to £120m; company-wide unexercised options from £3m to £6m; maximum exercise period from 10 years to 15, applying to existing options too — though documentation usually needs amending to use it. The individual limit is unchanged at £250,000. No tax on grant, none normally on exercise at market value, and BADR at 18% on sale after two years from grant without needing a 5% holding. Companies previously outside on size grounds should reconsider.

7 · Exit and succession

Business Asset Disposal Relief

Three changes in two years

  • 10% to 5 April 2025, 14% to 5 April 2026, 18% after — on a £1m lifetime limit. Investors’ Relief is aligned to the same schedule.
  • On a £1m qualifying gain the tax cost is now £80,000 higher than before the October 2024 Budget, purely from rate changes.
  • Pre-6 April 2026 contracts can keep the earlier rate, but only as an “excluded contract” — not created for timing advantage, wholly commercial where parties are connected, with a claim to HMRC. Signing before the deadline does not by itself guarantee it.
  • Two-year qualifying period, in every case. Share reorganisations, a new holding company, share class changes or a new shareholder can all restart clocks.

Business Property Relief — £2.5m, not £1m

Much commentary still quotes the old figure

  • From 6 April 2026, 100% BPR and APR is capped at a combined £2.5m per individual. Above that, 50% relief only — an effective 20% inheritance tax rate on the excess.
  • The path to the number. Announced at £1m in Autumn Budget 2024, raised to £2.5m on 23 December 2025, law on Royal Assent to the Finance Act 2026. HMRC’s estimate of affected estates fell from c.2,000 to c.1,100.
  • Transferable between spouses, and refreshing every seven years for individuals and ten for trusts — so £5m for a couple, more with nil-rate bands on top.
  • Anti-forestalling. Lifetime gifts made on or after 30 October 2024 come into the new regime if the donor dies on or after 6 April 2026 within seven years. Settle into more than one trust from that date and a single allowance is shared, not multiplied.
  • Wholly or mainly trading, and two years of ownership. Surplus cash, let property and investment portfolios inside the trading company risk diluting or failing that test.

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax

Began 6 April 2026 for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000, based on the 2024/25 return. The threshold falls to £30,000 from April 2027 and £20,000 from April 2028. Those in scope keep digital records and submit four quarterly updates plus a final declaration through compatible software. Qualifying income is gross self-employment and property income combined, before expenses; employment and pension income do not count. Limited companies and general partnerships are outside the regime for now.

The practical checklist

  • Map planned capital expenditure against the period end, the £1m AIA limit and the reduced 14% writing-down allowance — and check whether any asset likely to be sold on soon is better outside full expensing.
  • Confirm the fixed asset register records cost, date and which allowance was claimed, so balancing charges can be calculated correctly.
  • On any building project, have the expenditure analysed between structure and embedded plant before the accounts are finalised.
  • Model whether the merged R&D scheme or ERIS gives the better cash outcome, and diarise the six-month advance notification deadline if this is a first claim.
  • If the business holds or is filing patents, assess whether a Patent Box election is worth making.
  • Review director’s loan balances now against the nine-month deadline, and check whether interest at the official rate needs charging.
  • Record any year-end bonus formally before the period end and pay it within nine months; pay employer pension contributions in cleared funds before the year end, and check the 210% spreading test on any large one-off.
  • Count the associated companies and confirm the corporation tax, instalment and loss allowance thresholds that actually apply.
  • If profits are approaching £1.5m, model the cash flow impact of entering quarterly instalments before it happens.
  • Choose between current-year, carry-back and carry-forward loss relief by reference to the rate in each period — and specify the deductions allowance on the return.
  • Confirm the Employment Allowance is actually being claimed through payroll software.
  • Reassess whether EMI is now available under the widened April 2026 limits.
  • If a sale is on any horizon, confirm the BADR conditions are met, and have been for two years, before any reorganisation disturbs them.
  • Where combined business and agricultural assets exceed £2.5m, review ownership structure, trading status and succession plans.

Scope of this briefing

Covers the areas most commonly relevant to owner-managed UK trading companies. It does not address employee ownership trusts; group reorganisations, demergers and the substantial shareholding exemption; off-payroll working and employment status; VAT schemes, partial exemption and the construction industry reverse charge; property taxes including SDLT; international and transfer pricing matters; or the detailed conditions of EIS, SEIS and VCT investment. Each can materially change the analysis and warrants specific advice.
