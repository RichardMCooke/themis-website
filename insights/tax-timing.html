Six tax timing points UK SME owners are getting wrong in 2026

Most tax planning for an owner-managed business isn't structuring. It's sequencing — and several of the dates moved this year.

LinkedIn post · 6 Sep 2026

Most tax planning for an owner-managed business isn't structuring. It's sequencing — and several of the dates moved this year.

The six

01

Full expensing isn't automatically the right claim

100% relief looks obviously better than 14%. But dispose of an asset you've fully expensed and the whole disposal value returns as a balancing charge (CAA 2001 s59A) — a standalone taxable amount, not netted off the pool. For kit you'll sell on in a few years while it still holds value, AIA or writing-down allowances can leave you better off. The main pool WDA also fell from 18% to 14% on 1 April 2026.

02

ERIS isn't automatically better than the merged R&D scheme

ERIS is worth up to ~27p per £1 against the merged scheme's 14.7–16.2p. But it's only for loss-making SMEs at 30%+ R&D intensity, and that 27p assumes losses to surrender. Near break-even there's little to surrender and the flat merged rate can win.

Harder still: first-time claimants, or anyone who hasn't claimed in three years, must notify HMRC within six months of the period end. Miss it and the claim is gone.

03

An overdrawn director's loan carries two separate charges

s455 rose to 35.75% for loans outstanding from 6 April 2026. Repaying £5k+ then redrawing within 30 days is disregarded, and the refund doesn't arrive until nine months after the end of the period in which you repaid.

Separately: if the balance tops £10,000 at any point and you charge below HMRC's official rate (3.75%), that's a benefit in kind — income tax for you, Class 1A at 15% for the company. Charging interest fixes the second charge and does nothing for the first.

04

Two deductions depend on cash actually moving

Accrued remuneration is only deductible in the period accrued if paid within nine months of the year end (CTA 2009 s1288) — catching holiday pay accruals, not just director bonuses.

Employer pension contributions are deductible only when paid in cleared funds (FA 2004 s196). An accrual won't do.

05

Count your associated companies

The £50k/£250k corporation tax thresholds, the £1.5m instalment threshold and the £5m loss deductions allowance are all divided by associated companies plus one. The marginal rate between £50k and £250k is 26.5% — above the 25% headline — so deductible spend is worth more inside the band than above it.

06

Exit and succession got more expensive — but less than widely reported

BADR is 18% from 6 April 2026 on a £1m lifetime limit. BPR is now capped — but at £2.5m of combined business and agricultural property, not the £1m still being quoted. That was the original 2024 proposal, raised in December 2025 and now law. It's transferable between spouses, so £5m for a couple, with 50% relief above.

The point

None of this is exotic. It's the same decisions you were making anyway, with real cash attached to when you make them.
